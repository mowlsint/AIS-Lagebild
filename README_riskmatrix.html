## Quick Guide & Explanation

**What is this?**
This risk matrix is a working tool for a **structured initial assessment** of vessels in the context of maritime **hybrid threats** (e.g., shadow-fleet patterns, concealment, proximity to critical infrastructure, manipulation indicators). It does **not** replace situational analysis, evidence collection, or legal assessment — but it helps weight indicators consistently and compare multiple vessels in a repeatable way.

### 1) Core principle (Y × X)

The assessment uses two axes:

**Y-Axis: Structure (L1–L5)**
Describes how “concerning” a vessel is **structurally** — independent of current behavior. Examples: flag profile (Grey/Black/Unknown), frequent identity changes, opaque ownership/management chains, unusual insurance/class arrangements, sanctions links.

**X-Axis: Operational Indicators (X0–X5, shown as X0–X4 in the matrix)**
Describes the strength of **operational evidence** — what the vessel **does** or what anomalies/incident proximity exist (AIS blackout, GNSS/AIS spoofing, cable loitering, STS, “dark activity”, survey patterns, proximity to military, etc.).

**Important:** The table has a final column **“4/5 – Critical”**.
That means: X=4 or X=5 are both placed into the last column visually. Internally the finer “raw” value remains (e.g., X5), but the matrix display groups them (X4/5).

---

### 2) Data quality / Confidence (why it matters)

AIS and GNSS can be disrupted, manipulated, or incomplete. Therefore you can select **Data quality / Confidence**:

* **high (0.9):** AIS plus additional source(s) confirmed (e.g., imagery, port info, documents, OSINT verification).
* **medium (0.6):** AIS plausible, partly confirmed or consistent in context.
* **low (0.3):** AIS only / uncertain / significant ambiguity.

Confidence affects the **X-Axis**:
With low confidence, operational indicators are interpreted more cautiously to reduce false positives. (“AIS-only” patterns should not be rated like confirmed incidents.)

---

### 3) Step-by-step usage

**Step 1 — Basics:**
Enter vessel name, type, and flag state.
The selected flag automatically sets Grey/Black status (as an indicator display).

**Step 2 — Structure (Y):**
Tick structural indicators.
Rule of thumb: everything that belongs to the longer-term “profile” (ownership, flag changes, sanctions, class/insurance/identity churn) goes to Y.

**Step 3 — Operational indicators (X):**
Tick operational indicators.
Rule of thumb: current behavior, routes, patterns, incidents, and critical-infrastructure proximity go to X.

**Step 4 — Add vessel:**
Click **“Add vessel”**.
The vessel appears:
* as a **badge in the matrix cell** (Ly / X column)
* in the list below (risk level, X raw/matrix, confidence, top reasons)

**Step 5 — Details & notes:**
Click the badge (or the vessel entry in the list) to open the **details window**.
You can store an **editable free-text note** (e.g., sources, actions, assessment, open questions).
Notes are stored per vessel and can be overwritten anytime.

**Step 6 — Export:**
Use **“Export all as CSV”** to export the vessels for further processing.

---

### 4) Interpreting results (without fooling yourself)

* **Low / Medium** does not mean “safe” — it means: *currently no strong evidence or structurally unremarkable.*
* **High / Very high** means: *relevant indicators in structure and/or operational evidence — prioritize deeper checks.*
* A **high structure level (L4/L5)** without operational ticks can make sense (e.g., heavily obfuscated profile).
* A **high X score** should ideally be supported by **high confidence** — otherwise treat it as a working suspicion.

---

### 5) Typical pitfalls this matrix tries to prevent

* **“AIS says it, therefore it’s true.”** → confidence forces source discipline.
* **“One indicator = certainty.”** → X considers severity and combinations, not just the count.
* **“Flag alone decides everything.”** → flag influences Y, but operational evidence remains separate.
* **“Too many vessels, no overview.”** → matrix + list give visibility and comparability.

---

### 6) 20-second field note

1. Set flag + structural indicators (Y).
2. Set operational anomalies (X).
3. Choose realistic confidence.
4. Add vessel.
5. In the pop-up note: source + next action + open question.
